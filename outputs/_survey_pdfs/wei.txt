UAV’s Rotor Micro-Doppler Feature Extraction
Using Integrated Sensing and Communication
Signal: Algorithm Design and
Testbed Evaluation
Jiachen Wei , Dingyou Ma , Feiyang He, Qixun Zhang , Member, IEEE, Zhiyong Feng , Zhengfeng Liu,
and Taohong Liang
Abstract—With the rapid application of uncrewed aerial vehi-
cles (UAVs) in urban areas, the identiﬁcation and tracking of
hovering UAVs have become critical challenges, signiﬁcantly
impacting the safety of aircraft take-off and landing operations.
As a promising technology for 6G mobile systems integrated
sensing and communication (ISAC), can be used to detect high-
mobility UAVs with a low deployment cost. The micro-Doppler
signals from UAV rotors can be leveraged to address the detection
of low-mobility and hovering UAVs using ISAC signals. However,
determining whether the frame structure of the ISAC system can
be used to identify UAVs, and how to accurately capture the weak
rotor micro-Doppler signals of UAVs in complex environments,
remain two challenging problems. This paper ﬁrst proposes a
novel frame structure for UAV micro-Doppler extraction and the
representation of UAV micro-Doppler signals within the channel
state information (CSI). Furthermore, to address complex envi-
ronments and the interference caused by UAV body vibrations,
the rotor micro-Doppler null space pursuit (rmD-NSP) algorithm
and the feature extraction algorithm synchroextracting transform
(SET) are designed to effectively separate UAV’s rotor micro-
Doppler signals and enhance their features in the spectrogram.
Finally, both simulation and hardware testbed demonstrate that
the proposed rmD-NSP algorithm enables the ISAC base station
(BS) to accurately and completely extract UAV’s rotor micro-
Doppler signals. Within the observation period of 0.1 s, ISAC
BS successfully captures eight rotations of the DJI M300 RTK
UAV’s rotor in urban environments. Compared to the existing
AM-FM NSP, NSP, MTD, EMD and VMD signal decomposition
algorithms, the integrity of the rotor micro-Doppler features is
improved by 60%.

I. INTRODUCTION
T
HE rapid development of 5G mobile communication
systems and artiﬁcial intelligence technology has drawn
signiﬁcant attention to the low-altitude economy [1]. The low
cost, small size, ease of acquisition, and particularly live video
feed have greatly popularized the use of uncrewed aerial
vehicles (UAVs). Unfortunately, the increasing popularity of
UAV applications has raised concerns about safety, security,
and privacy, mainly due to the so-called smart attacks and
threats [2]. It is critically important to ﬁnd ways to counter the
illegal UAVs intrusion [3]. Therefore, numerous researchers
from academia and industry have tried to develop suitable
UAV detection systems, primarily through the use of multiple
sensors, such as ultrasonic sensors, light detection and rang-
ing (LiDAR), optical cameras, millimeter wave (mmWave)
radars, etc. At the same time, the next generation of wireless
networks, in addition to providing primary communication
functions, is also expected to gain the capability to sense
the surrounding environment through radio frequency (RF)
signals, effectively acting as a sensor [4]. Leveraging existing
communication base stations (BSs) and pipeline infrastructure,
a UAV detection network can be rapidly and cost-effectively
constructed. Furthermore, the inherent network collaborative
capability of multiple BSs can enhance the detection accu-
racy and reduce blind spot detection, which can address the
problem of UAV being difﬁcult to detect in urban scenarios
[5].
As one of the key technologies for 6G communications,
integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) systems provide
theoretical feasibility for using mobile communication systems
to detect UAVs [6]. Current research on ISAC primarily
focuses on four aspects: signal design [7], [8], [9], [10], signal
processing [11], [12], [13], [14], protocol-based applications
[15], [16], [17], [18], and network sensing [19], [20], [21].
In scenarios where multiple BSs are used for UAV detection,
current research mainly focuses on UAV localization and
tracking [22], [23], [24]. Speciﬁcally, these ISAC signal pro-
cessing algorithms assume that the UAV is a point target with
strong reﬂections and high-speed movement characteristics.
These assumptions do not align with actual conditions [25],

[26], [27], [28]. First, the assumption that the UAV is a single
scatterer neglects the reﬂections from the rotor blades. Second,
UAVs typically move at a slower speed and can hover, making
them difﬁcult to detect from clutter, noise, and other natural
ﬂying objects such as birds in urban environments. Actually,
the rotation of the UAV’s rotor introduces unique high-
frequency modulation sidebands near the Doppler frequency,
known as the micro-Doppler signal. This micro-Doppler signal
can be used to identify the type of target and distinguish it
from other objects [29].
However, existing ISAC studies do not consider the appli-
cation of micro-Doppler to detect and identify UAV in mobile
communication systems. There remains a lack of practical
performance evaluation for using mobile communication sys-
tems to extract the micro-Doppler signatures of UAVs. The
challenges with micro-Doppler extraction of UAV based on
mobile communication systems come from two main sources.
One aspect is whether the communication waveform and
uplink/downlink frame structure affect the extraction of UAV
micro-Doppler features. In addition, considering the complex
environment faced by micro-Doppler signal extraction, there
is a signiﬁcant amount of clutter and interference. The UAV’s
rotor echoes to be extracted are very weak, making the
extraction of micro-Doppler features challenging. Moreover,
the vibration of UAVs itself also affects the performance of
micro-Doppler extraction [30], [31].
Therefore, to address the above issues, this paper presents a
time division duplexing (TDD) frame structure conﬁguration
for extracting micro-Doppler features of UAV using ISAC
signals. Furthermore, it provides an expression for the UAV
echoes in the context of channel state information (CSI).
This expression accounts for the time-varying radar cross
section (RCS) induced by the UAV’s rotor rotation, which
leads to periodic variations in rotor echo intensity. Addition-
ally, the model incorporates the translational and vibrational
motion of the UAV body. To combat the complex environment
and suppress the effect of body vibrations, the rotor micro-
Doppler null space pursuit (rmD-NSP) algorithm is designed,
based on an operator-based signal decomposition method
[32], [33], [34], to extract weak rotor micro-Doppler signals
from various interference sources. The rotor micro-Doppler
signals obtained from decomposition can be further processed
using the synchroextracting transform (SET) algorithm to
obtain time-frequency ridges with more concentrated energy,
thereby revealing more pronounced micro-Doppler features
[35]. Finally, both simulation and hardware testbed results
demonstrate that the proposed rmD-NSP algorithm extracts
UAV rotor micro-Doppler signals in urban environments more
effectively than existing AM-FM NSP [34] and NSP [32],
MTD [36], EMD [37] and VMD [38] signal decomposition
algorithms. The main contributions of this paper are summa-
rized as follows.
• A new mathematical model for UAV micro-Doppler in
monostatic ISAC systems is proposed. This paper pro-
vides a model for the representation of the micro-Doppler
signal in the CSI and considers the rotation of multiple
UAV rotor blades, as well as the translational motion and
vibration of the UAV body during ﬂight. Additionally, the
mathematical model takes into account the dynamic RCS
of the rotors caused by blade rotation, which will be an
interesting tool for the future development of ISAC target
classiﬁcation algorithms.
• We design an rmD-NSP adaptive operator based on
the mathematical model of the UAV rotor. Through
the null space tracking algorithm, we extract rotor
micro-Doppler signals from mixed signals that con-
tain multiple interference components. In addition, we
use the time-frequency enhancement algorithm SET to
complete the extraction of the micro-Doppler feature
of UAVs.
• Through software simulations and hardware testbed
experiments based on the ISAC system, we validate the
effectiveness of the rmD-NSP algorithm and the SET
algorithm to extract the micro-Doppler feature of UAVs in
mobile communication systems. Within the observation
period of 0.1 s, ISAC BS successfully captures eight
rotations of the DJI M300 RTK UAV’s rotor in an urban
environment.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section II
presents the system model of the UAV micro-Doppler feature
extraction. Section III introduces the rmD-NSP algorithm and
the SET algorithm. In Section IV, we present the software sim-
ulation results. In Section V, we implement the extraction of
micro-Doppler features from UAVs using the ISAC hardware
testbed. Section VI concludes this paper.

Boldface lowercase and uppercase letters denote vectors and
matrices, respectively. We denote the transpose operation
as (·)T. Ax = diag(x) denotes the diagonalization of the

II. SYSTEM MODEL
In this section, we consider an ISAC BS to implement
the micro-Doppler feature extraction function for UAV, as
shown in Fig. 1(a). The BS is equipped with separate antennas
that enable full-duplex operation, allowing it to transmit and
receive signals simultaneously.
First, in Subsection II-A, we present a frame structure
based on 3GPP TS 38.211 [39] for extracting UAV micro-
Doppler features using the BS, as shown in Fig. 1(b).
In Subsection II-B, we present the ISAC echo representation of
the UAV, taking into account the dynamic RCS characteristics
and the rotational motion of the rotor blades, as well as the
translational and vibrational motion of the UAV body. Then,
in Subsection II-C, the CSI is provided, which contains target
range, Doppler, and micro-Doppler information.

The ISAC system proposed in this paper is based on the
5G new radio (NR) protocol. The 5G NR basic resource
unit in the time domain is the radio frame, with each radio
frame lasting for 10 ms and consisting of 10 subframes,
each with a duration of 1 ms. With the subcarrier spacing

Micro-Doppler feature extraction requires the sensing sym-

consider a frame structure designed for UAV micro-Doppler
feature extraction as shown in Fig. 1(b). During DL slots, the
BS utilizes all physical downlink shared channel (PDSCH)
signals as sensing symbols, resulting in the shortest pulse
repetition interval (PRI) of 33.3 µs. Currently, BS operates
in a TDD mode with a single cycle duration of 2.5 ms, i.e.,
“DDDSU”, as the ISAC mode. After BS detects an unknown
ﬂying target, it transmits communication signals to the tar-
get in the DL slots while simultaneously receiving echoes.
In this sensing mode, the extracted micro-Doppler features
are unambiguous.

III. UAV ROTOR FEATURE EXTRACTION ALGORITHM
In this section, we present a method for extracting UAV
micro-Doppler features using an ISAC BS. The overall process
is illustrated in Fig. 4. First, in Subsection III-A, we introduce
the matched ﬁltering method to determine the UAV’s position
and provide the mixed signal model for decomposition. Then
in Subsection III-B, an operator-based signal decomposition
method, denoted by rmD-NSP, is adopted to extract the rotor
micro-Doppler signals from the mixed signals. Finally, in
Subsection III-C, the feature extraction method, denoted by
SET, is applied to obtain the high resolution rotor features
from the decomposed signals.

B. Operator-Based Signal Decomposition Algorithm
In Section III-A, we obtain a mixed signal smix that
contains the rotor micro-Doppler signals d(kp)krotation

along

mD

features, as shown in Fig. 5. This task is essentially a multi-
component signal decomposition problem. Although several
multi-component signal decomposition algorithms-such as
empirical mode decomposition (EMD) and variational mode
decomposition (VMD) [37], [38]–have been studied, they
do not decompose the signal based on the speciﬁc form of
the target, making them unsuitable for extracting the micro-
Doppler signals from smix.
Null space pursuit (NSP) is an adaptive operator-based
signal decomposition method that constructs adaptive oper-
ators to remove local narrowband signals in the null space
for separation [32], [33], [34]. The main appeal of this
algorithm lies in its ability to design an adaptive operator based
on the target signal model, thereby enabling the extraction
of the target signal from the mixed signal. The algorithm
implementation consists of two main steps: (i) designing a
parameterized operator based on the target signal form, and
(ii) adaptively adjusting the operator parameters via null-space
tracking while simultaneously extracting the target signal.

A. Comparison of Micro-Doppler Features Under Different
Frame Structures
The conﬁguration of the frame structure directly inﬂuences
the density of sensing symbols, which is critical for extracting
unblurred UAV micro-Doppler features. To ensure that these
features are preserved, the pulse repetition frequency (PRF)
must be at least twice the maximum micro-Doppler frequency,
i.e., PRF ≥2f max
mD , where the maximum micro-Doppler
frequency is given by f max
mD
=
2πfrL
λ
. For example, using
our hardware test parameters (fr = 80 r/s and L = 0.5 m),
we obtain fmD = 2934 Hz, which requires a PRF of at least
5868 Hz (corresponding to a PRI of less than 1.7 × 10−4 s).
If DMRS signals are used as sensing symbols [44],
[45], [46], the downlink sensing symbol interval would be
1×10−4 s, which might appear to satisfy the PRI requirement.
However, because the complete ISAC process includes uplink
slots-during which no sensing symbols are transmitted-the
overall sensing symbol density is insufﬁcient for accurate
micro-Doppler extraction.
To overcome this limitation, we propose the DDDSU frame
structure as shown in Fig. 1(b), in which all PDSCH signals
in the downlink slots are employed as sensing symbols. This
conﬁguration achieves a downlink sensing symbol interval
of 3.33 × 10−5 s, well below the required PRI. Although
uplink slots do not carry sensing symbols, both simulations
and hardware tests conﬁrm that the overall sensing symbol
density is adequate for extracting clear UAV micro-Doppler
features. While this sensing mode sacriﬁces some communi-
cation resources, it is essential for meeting the stringent PRI
requirements necessary for effective micro-Doppler extraction.

APPENDIX A
COMPUTATIONAL COMPLEXITY OF
THE PROPOSED ALGORITHM
The computational complexity of the rmD-NSP algorithm
can be derived based on the implementation steps provided
in Section III-B. Each iteration of the algorithm consists
of four steps. Speciﬁcally, updating the operator parameters
θj according to (32), the Lagrange parameters λj
1 according
to (33), and the residual components rj+1 according to (35)
requires matrix multiplication and matrix inversion, resulting
in a computational complexity of O(M 3) (j denotes the index
of iteration and M denotes the number of OFDM symbols
used for sensing). Additionally, updating the leakage param-
eter γj+1 according to (36) involves computing vector inner
products and norms, leading to a computational complexity
of O(M). Therefore, the overall computational complexity of
the proposed algorithm is O(M 3 + M).
During our hardware tests, the value of M was 1680.
We conducted algorithm runtime simulations on a machine
equipped with a 13th Gen Intel R
⃝CoreTM i9-13900HX proces-
sor at 2.20 GHz with 16.0 GB of RAM (15.8 GB available).
The experimental results show that the algorithm execution
time is approximately 0.84 seconds. These results indicate
that, while the algorithm is computationally intensive, it is
feasible for real-time applications on state-of-the-art hardware.
Moreover, further optimizations such as parallel processing or
implementation on dedicated hardware (e.g., FPGA or GPU)
can further enhance its real-time performance.
